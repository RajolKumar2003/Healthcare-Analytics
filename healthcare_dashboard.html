Executive Summary

What leadership needs to know, at a glance.
1

The network processed 54,966 patient episodes generating an estimated $1.41B in aggregate billing over the five-year window — with average cost per patient holding steady near $25,600.

2

Case mix is unusually balanced: all six tracked conditions (Arthritis, Diabetes, Hypertension, Obesity, Cancer, Asthma) sit within a tight 9,095–9,218 patient band — no single condition dominates capacity.

3

Billing shows no age-based cost gradient — senior patients (60+) average $25,564 versus $25,595 network-wide, meaning cost is driven by condition/procedure, not patient age.

4

Payer mix is well diversified across 5 insurers (19.7%–20.3% share each), limiting reimbursement-concentration risk with any single provider.

5

6,038 emergency patients (11.0% of the network) presented with abnormal test results — a sizeable, ongoing clinical-review workload that warrants a standing triage process.

6

Average monthly billing has stayed inside a narrow $24,900–$26,300 band across five years — costs are stable with no inflationary trend detected in the data.

Key Business Insights Core Analysis

Findings behind the network's five core performance questions.

Where does patient volume concentrate?

Arthritis (9,218 patients) is marginally the most common condition network-wide, but every tracked condition falls within a narrow 1.3% band of one another.

Implication: No condition-specific bottleneck exists — clinical staffing and equipment budgets can be allocated evenly across Arthritis, Diabetes, Hypertension, Obesity, Cancer and Asthma service lines.

Which payers cover the network?

Coverage is split almost equally across five providers, led narrowly by Cigna (20.3%).

Implication: No single payer holds negotiating leverage over the network — contract terms can be optimized independently across all five relationships.

Is treatment cost rising over time?

Monthly average billing has held flat since 2019.

Implication: Pricing and cost controls are working — no runaway cost inflation to flag to finance.

Does age drive treatment cost?

Senior (60+) billing is statistically flat versus the network average.

Management Takeaways

What's happening, why it matters, and where to focus next.
Operations

Case mix is balanced — resource evenly

No condition line exceeds 16.8% of volume. Staffing, equipment and bed allocation can follow a standardized model across Arthritis, Diabetes, Hypertension, Obesity, Cancer and Asthma rather than over-indexing on one specialty.

Finance

Costs are stable — no inflation signal

Average billing has held inside a $24,900–$26,300 band for five straight years and does not rise with patient age. Cost-containment efforts should target procedure and condition mix, not demographic risk-adjustment.

Risk & Payer Strategy

Payer diversification limits exposure

Five insurers each hold 19.7%–20.3% of coverage. No renegotiation with a single payer can materially disrupt revenue — a favorable position for contract leverage.

Staffing

Plan for a July emergency-volume peak

Emergency admissions rise ~8% from the February low to the July high. Proactive seasonal staffing ahead of summer can smooth the surge.

Quality & Safety

Formalize the abnormal-results triage queue

6,038 emergency patients (11.0% of the network) carried abnormal test results. A standing fast-track clinical review protocol would ensure timely follow-up on this at-risk group.

Finance

Keep the high-cost cohort under standing review

5,497 patients (top billing decile) should remain a recurring monthly finance review to catch billing anomalies and validate documentation early.
